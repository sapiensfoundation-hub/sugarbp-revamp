People with diabetes

Who is a diabetic?

Diabetes is the other leading cause of chronic kidney disease. It's a chronic condition where the body produces or uses insulin poorly — and over years, it quietly affects the heart, kidneys, eyes and nerves.

The definition

A person is considered diabetic if, on two separate occasions, any of the following values are present:

  • Fasting blood sugar greater than 126 mg%, or
  • Random blood sugar greater than 200 mg%, or
  • Blood sugar 2 hours after 75 grams of glucose (post-prandial, PP) greater than 200 mg%

Normal fasting blood sugar should be below 110 mg%, and post-prandial (PP) sugar should be below 140 mg%. If a reading falls between the normal and diabetic values, the person is called pre-diabetic.

Why it matters for your kidneys

Diabetes is a chronic disease involving insufficient insulin production or utilisation. Left uncontrolled, it leads to long-term complications involving the heart, kidneys, eyes and nerves. Diabetic kidney damage, like hypertensive damage, develops silently — annual screening is the only reliable way to catch it early.

Reduced vision from retinopathy can be an early warning sign of kidney damage in a diabetic individual, since both are caused by the same small-vessel damage.
