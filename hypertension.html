People with hypertension

Who has high blood pressure?

High blood pressure is one of the two leading causes of chronic kidney disease. Knowing exactly where your reading falls is the first step toward protecting your kidneys.

The definition

Any adult over 20 years of age with a systolic blood pressure of 140 mmHg or more, and a diastolic blood pressure of 90 mmHg or more, recorded on two separate occasions in a relaxed sitting position, is considered hypertensive.

A systolic reading between 120 and 139, or a diastolic reading between 80 and 89, is considered pre-hypertensive and should be monitored for hypertension in the future.

The American Heart Association, in 2018, classified a systolic reading of 130–140 with a diastolic reading of 80–89 as Stage I hypertension.

Why it matters for your kidneys

Sustained high blood pressure damages the small blood vessels inside the kidneys over time, reducing their ability to filter waste. This is one of the two most common paths to chronic kidney disease (CKD) — and like CKD itself, it produces no symptoms until a great deal of damage has already occurred.

The only defence is testing on a schedule. Check your blood pressure at least once a year, even if you feel completely well.
